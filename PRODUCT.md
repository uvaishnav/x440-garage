# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The sole user is the owner of a Harley-Davidson X440 Matte Denim Black. The app is used one-handed on an iPhone at petrol bunks, before and after highway trips, during routine garage care, and when handing the bike over for service. The primary jobs are fast fuel capture, trustworthy mileage analysis, maintenance reminders, trip preparation, and retaining issues and ownership costs without relying on memory.

## Product Purpose

X440 Garage is a personal digital cockpit and service record for keeping one X440 reliable and enjoyable over a long ownership period. Success means a fuel fill can be logged in seconds, maintenance due by kilometres or time is hard to overlook, highway trips can be prepared and analysed separately, and every useful service detail remains available when speaking to a technician.

## Brand Personality

Precise, mechanical, premium. The product should feel calm and deliberate like a well-finished motorcycle instrument cluster, with the directness and ergonomic restraint of a highly rated native iPhone utility.

## Anti-references

- Generic dashboard templates with interchangeable cards and decorative charts.
- Novelty motorcycle styling, flames, fake carbon fibre, chrome effects, or gaming aesthetics.
- Emoji, mismatched icon packs, excessive gradients, gratuitous motion, or dense engineering jargon.
- Interfaces that hide primary actions, require two-handed use at a petrol bunk, or present estimates as exact measurements.

## Design Principles

1. Make the frequent action immediate: fuel logging always begins from the centre action and asks only for information that improves the record.
2. Distinguish exact measurements from estimates so mileage and cost insights remain trustworthy.
3. Reveal detail progressively: the cockpit shows decisions and warnings; history and analysis sit one level deeper.
4. Let the bike shape the information architecture: fuel, trips, care, issues, and service records should feel like one ownership system.
5. Prefer familiar iPhone behavior, readable numbers, and generous touch targets over decorative novelty.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The app targets an iPhone-sized touch surface with at least 44-point controls, safe-area support, 16-pixel form inputs, clear non-color status labels, sufficient dark-mode contrast, semantic labels, and reduced-motion alternatives. It must remain usable outdoors, at night, and with one hand.
